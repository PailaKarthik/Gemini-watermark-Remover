What is the Gemini watermark?

Images and videos generated with Google's Gemini models often arrive with a visible badge stamped in the corner — the "Gemini watermark". Here's what it is, and what removing it does and doesn't mean.

Two different watermarks

👁

The visible badge

A small semi-transparent sparkle logo + "Gemini" text rendered into the pixels, usually bottom-left or bottom-right. This is what people mean by "the Gemini watermark", and it's what our tool removes.

🔎

SynthID (invisible)

Google's SynthID embeds a hidden, imperceptible statistical signature into the pixel data itself. It isn't something you can see or crop out, and it's designed to survive edits like re-encoding.

What removal can and cannot do

  • Can: erase the visible badge with texture-aware inpainting so the corner looks natural again — at original resolution/quality, editing only the pixels you select.
  • Cannot (and won't pretend to): strip invisible SynthID. That's a statistical property spread across the whole image, not a corner graphic. No honest tool claims to reliably remove it.

Why the badge exists

The visible mark helps viewers recognize AI-generated media at a glance. That's a reasonable goal — but when you generated the image for your own project, a permanent corner stamp can be limiting for thumbnails, design work, or video frames where the badge distracts. Removing the badge from media you own and created is a personal-choice use case; our tool exists for exactly that.

Responsible use

Don't use watermark removal to pass off other people's AI content as untouched original work, to evade labeling requirements, or to mislead anyone about a media file's origin. Use it on content you own or have rights to modify. See our terms of use.
